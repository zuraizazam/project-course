# Design and Performance Optimization of a Relational Database for an Electric Vehicle Charging Network with AI Support

## My interpretation of the brief

My understanding of this project is that the main objective is to design and implement a relational database for an electric vehicle charging network and then investigate and improve its performance using measurement-based database optimization techniques.

The system needs to represent the main business and technical entities of a charging network, including charging stations, charging points, users, vehicles, reservations, charging sessions, energy measurements, tariffs, payments, and technical events. The database must be designed to at least third normal form and must enforce important integrity and business rules at the database level.

A major part of the project is the generation of a large reproducible test dataset. The database should contain at least one hundred thousand charging sessions and at least one million timestamped measurement or status records. This provides a realistic basis for analyzing the performance of complex SQL queries.

I understand that performance optimization should be based on actual measurements and execution plans rather than assumptions. At least five analytical queries need to be investigated before and after optimization, including the effects of simple and composite indexes and, where appropriate, database-specific indexing techniques.

Another interesting aspect of the project is the use of an external AI service to provide optimization or indexing suggestions. These suggestions must not simply be accepted automatically. They need to be reviewed, tested, and compared against solutions developed independently, with their correctness, executability, and measured performance documented.

## Proposed approach

I would first analyze the requirements and design a normalized relational model covering charging stations, charging points, users, vehicles, reservations, charging sessions, energy consumption measurements, tariffs, payments, and technical events.

I would then implement the database using Microsoft SQL Server, using T-SQL for database-side logic. Important relationships and business rules would be enforced through primary keys, foreign keys, unique constraints, checks, stored procedures, functions, and triggers where justified.

A custom test-data generation component would be developed to produce at least one hundred thousand charging sessions and at least one million timestamped measurements or status records. The generation process would be deterministic or reproducible so that performance experiments can be repeated.

I would create at least ten complex SQL queries representing realistic operational and business tasks. These would include multi-table joins, aggregation, time-interval searches, subqueries or CTEs, and window functions.

For at least five queries, I would record baseline execution plans and runtime characteristics before optimization. I would then investigate indexing and query-optimization alternatives, including composite and filtered indexes where appropriate for Microsoft SQL Server. The effects would be measured quantitatively and the execution-plan changes would be documented.

For at least three selected queries, I would additionally ask an external AI service to suggest indexing or query-optimization solutions. The suggestions would be reviewed manually and tested rather than executed automatically. I would compare them with my own solutions in terms of correctness, executability, execution plan, and measured performance.
